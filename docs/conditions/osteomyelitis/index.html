Osteomyelitis (bone infection)

Infection inside a bone, often after a wound, surgery or a long-standing ulcer.

What it is

Osteomyelitis is an infection inside a bone. Bacteria reach the bone through a wound or ulcer that goes deep enough, through surgery or a fracture, or occasionally through the bloodstream. Because bone has a limited blood supply, antibiotics reach it slowly and the infection can smolder for weeks or months. In Hawaiʻi we most often see it in the feet of people with diabetes, in the spine, and after orthopedic surgery.

How we diagnose it

We look at the wound and the bone around it, and usually order imaging — an X-ray first, often an MRI. Blood tests show how much inflammation there is. The most useful test is a sample of the bone itself, taken by a surgeon or a radiologist, because it tells us exactly which organism is present and which antibiotics will work. We try to get that sample before antibiotics start whenever it is safe to wait.

How we treat it

Treatment is a course of antibiotics chosen for the specific organism, usually lasting six weeks or more. Many patients need IV antibiotics for part or all of that time, which we provide at home through our infusion program rather than in the hospital. Some infections also need surgery to remove dead bone or hardware; we work with your surgeon on the timing. We check labs regularly during treatment to watch for side effects and to confirm the infection is responding.

What to expect

Improvement is gradual: pain and drainage ease over the first weeks, and blood tests fall toward normal. We see you in clinic during treatment and again after antibiotics stop, because a small number of bone infections return and are easier to treat when caught early. Good blood sugar control, pressure relief for foot ulcers, and not smoking all improve the chance of a lasting cure.

Call us if

  • New or worsening pain, redness or swelling around the bone
  • Drainage from a wound that had been closing
  • Fever or chills
  • Any side effect from your antibiotic, such as rash, diarrhea or nausea that keeps you from eating
