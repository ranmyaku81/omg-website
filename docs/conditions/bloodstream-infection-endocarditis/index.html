Bloodstream infection and endocarditis

Bacteria in the blood, and infection of the heart valves.

What it is

A bloodstream infection (bacteremia) means bacteria are circulating in the blood. It usually starts somewhere else — a urinary infection, a skin infection, an IV line, a dental source — and can seed other sites. Endocarditis is infection of the heart valves or the lining of the heart, and is the most serious form, because bacteria growing on a valve can damage it and break off into the circulation.

How we diagnose it

Blood cultures identify the organism. Depending on which bacteria is found, we look for where it came from and where it may have spread: an ultrasound of the heart (echocardiogram), imaging of the spine or other sites if there is pain, and repeat blood cultures to confirm the blood has cleared. Certain bacteria, especially Staphylococcus aureus, always get a full evaluation because they so often involve the heart or bone.

How we treat it

Treatment is IV antibiotics, usually started in the hospital and continued at home through our infusion program. Length depends on the organism and whether the heart or bone is involved: two weeks for a simple bloodstream infection, four to six weeks for endocarditis or a deep source. Some patients with endocarditis need heart surgery to repair or replace a damaged valve; we work closely with cardiology and cardiac surgery on that decision. Removing the source — an infected line, an abscess — is as important as the antibiotic.

What to expect

We see you in clinic during home therapy to review labs, check the IV line and watch for complications, and again after treatment ends. If you have had endocarditis, you will need antibiotics before certain dental procedures in the future, and we will give you a card explaining this.

Call us if

  • Fever or chills returning after they had settled
  • New shortness of breath, chest pain, or swelling of the legs
  • Sudden weakness, trouble speaking, vision change or severe headache — call 911
  • New back pain, joint pain or a painful area under the skin
