Prosthetic joint infection

Infection around an artificial hip, knee or shoulder.

What it is

An artificial joint can become infected soon after surgery or years later, when bacteria from elsewhere in the body settle on the implant. Bacteria form a coating called biofilm on the metal and plastic that protects them from antibiotics and the immune system, which is why these infections rarely clear with antibiotics alone.

How we diagnose it

Pain in a joint that had been working well, swelling, warmth, drainage or a wound that will not heal are the usual signs. We check blood inflammation markers and, most importantly, sample fluid from the joint so the organism can be grown in the laboratory. Imaging helps show whether the implant has loosened.

How we treat it

Treatment is a partnership between your orthopedic surgeon and us. Depending on how long the infection has been present and how well the implant is fixed, the surgeon may wash out the joint and exchange the plastic parts, or remove and later replace the implant. We direct the antibiotics around that surgery: usually several weeks of IV therapy, often given at home, sometimes followed by a longer course of pills. Which antibiotic and for how long depends on the organism grown from the joint.

What to expect

Recovery takes months and involves both infection treatment and rehabilitation. We follow you in clinic through the antibiotic course and afterwards. Most patients keep a functioning joint; the chance of cure is highest when infection is recognized early, so do not wait on a joint that has become newly painful.

Call us if

  • New pain, swelling or warmth in a replaced joint
  • Drainage or an opening in the surgical scar
  • Fever
  • Antibiotic side effects
