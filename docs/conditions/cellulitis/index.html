Cellulitis and skin infections

Infection of the skin and the tissue beneath it, including abscesses and infections that keep returning.

What it is

Cellulitis is a bacterial infection of the skin and the layer beneath it: an area that is red, warm, swollen and tender, often on the lower leg. Bacteria enter through a crack, scratch, insect bite, athlete’s foot or a wound. An abscess is a collection of pus under the skin. Most cases are treated by primary care; we see people whose infection is severe, is not responding, keeps coming back, or occurs in someone with a weakened immune system.

How we diagnose it

The diagnosis is usually made by examination. We look for the entry point, for pus that needs draining, and for conditions that mimic cellulitis, such as a blood clot, gout, or inflammation from poor circulation, which are often mistaken for infection and treated with antibiotics that do not help. Cultures are useful when there is pus or an open wound.

How we treat it

Uncomplicated cellulitis responds to five to ten days of oral antibiotics; abscesses need to be drained. Severe infections, or infections in people who cannot take pills, are treated with IV antibiotics, which can often be given at home. For people with repeated episodes, we treat the underlying cause — chronic leg swelling, athlete’s foot, skin breakdown — and sometimes use a low-dose preventive antibiotic.

What to expect

Redness often spreads slightly in the first day of treatment before it improves, and can take one to two weeks to fade fully. Elevating the leg speeds recovery. We follow up to confirm the infection has cleared and to put prevention in place if this has happened before.

Call us if

  • Redness spreading quickly, or streaks running up the limb
  • Severe pain out of proportion to what you see, or skin turning dark or blistering — go to an emergency department
  • Fever, confusion or feeling very unwell
  • No improvement after two days of antibiotics
