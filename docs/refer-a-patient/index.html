For referring clinicians

Outpatient infectious disease consultation in Honolulu and Hilo, hospital consults across Oʻahu and at Hilo Benioff Medical Center, and a home IV antibiotic (OPAT) program run by our own nurses.

How to refer

  1. Complete the one-page referral form, or send your own with a reason for consultation.
  2. Fax to (808) 528-5507 with the items below.
  3. We call the patient to schedule and fax your office the consult note after the visit. For urgent problems, call (808) 531-7111 and ask for the physician on call.

What to include

The more of this we have before the visit, the less often we have to repeat testing or call your office:

  • Most recent progress note and problem list, with the specific question you want answered
  • Culture results with susceptibilities (blood, urine, wound, bone, joint fluid)
  • Imaging reports, and the facility where images can be retrieved
  • Recent labs: CBC, creatinine, CRP or ESR, liver enzymes if on antimicrobials
  • Antibiotics given so far, with dates and doses
  • Allergies and reactions, and current medication list
  • Insurance information and the patient’s best phone number

When a referral helps most

  • Bone and joint infections, including diabetic foot
  • Prosthetic joint or hardware infections
  • Bacteremia and endocarditis
  • Infections failing first-line therapy
  • Recurrent UTI with resistant organisms
  • Any patient who needs IV antibiotics at home
  • HIV: new diagnosis, care transfer, or PrEP
  • Nontuberculous mycobacteria
  • Fungal infections
  • Fever of unknown origin
  • Infection in immunocompromised patients
  • Pre-travel or post-travel evaluation

Not sure a formal consult is needed? Call the office; a brief physician-to-physician conversation is often enough to answer a question about antibiotic choice or duration.

Discharging a patient on IV antibiotics

For hospitalists and discharge planners: our OPAT program takes over the infusion, nursing visits, weekly labs, line care and physician follow-up through end of therapy, on Oʻahu and in East Hawaiʻi. To make the first home dose happen on the day of discharge, we need:

  • Infectious disease consult note with the antimicrobial, dose, frequency and planned stop date
  • Vascular access placed and documented (PICC or midline, tip position confirmed)
  • Most recent susceptibilities, creatinine and weight
  • Discharge address, phone, and who will help with infusions at home
  • Insurance information for authorization
  • 24 hours’ notice where possible; same-day discharges can usually be covered on Oʻahu with a morning call

Call (808) 531-7111 and ask for the infusion team, or fax the above to (808) 528-5507 marked “OPAT”.

Hospital consults

For inpatients, place a consult order in your hospital’s system or page the infectious disease physician on service through the operator. We cover Queen’s, Queen’s – West Oʻahu, Kuakini, Straub, Pali Momi and Adventist Health Castle on Oʻahu, and Dr. Thomas consults at Hilo Benioff Medical Center.

What you get back

A consult note after each visit with the diagnosis, antimicrobial plan and stop date, monitoring labs we are following, and when we will see the patient again. We copy you on significant changes.

Turnaround

Urgent referrals are seen within a few days; routine referrals typically within one to two weeks. Tell us on the form if the patient is being discharged or is deteriorating.
